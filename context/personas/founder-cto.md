# Persona: Founder / CTO

---

## Who They Are

**Titles:** Founder, Co-Founder, CTO, Founder/CTO
**Company size:** Typically 10–150 employees; engineering team of 3–30
**Background:** Often the first engineer or first hire; still writes code or is deeply embedded in tech decisions
**Decision style:** Fast, intuition-driven, low process — if the product is good, they'll know in 15 minutes

**ICP examples from our accounts:**
- Founder/CTO at Empora (35 employees)
- Founder at Blitz Insurance (41 employees, 7 engineers)
- Founder/CTO at hypercourt (15 employees)
- Founder at delago.com (20 employees)

---

## What They Care About

### Primary Goals
1. **Shipping speed:** They feel every day of delay personally. Speed = competitive advantage.
2. **Not paying for waste:** CI/CD is usually their 2nd or 3rd biggest infrastructure bill. It bothers them.
3. **Not managing infrastructure:** They don't want to think about CI/CD. It should just work.
4. **Engineer happiness:** Tiny teams can't afford attrition. If engineers hate their tools, it's a real problem.

### How They Measure Success
- "Can we ship more often?" (daily deploys vs. weekly)
- Monthly CI/CD spend line item in their AWS/infrastructure budget
- How long engineers wait for feedback after a commit
- How many hours/week the team spends babysitting pipelines

### Decision Criteria
- **They are the decision maker.** No internal selling required.
- **Speed of evaluation:** They'll decide in 1–2 conversations if the PoC shows what it claims
- **Trust:** They respond to founder-to-founder credibility. Dan Manges = Root Insurance CTO = credibility.
- **Simplicity:** If it's too complicated to set up, they'll move on

---

## How They Buy

- Decision happens in days, not weeks — if they're excited, they move fast
- Budget authority: Unlimited within reason (it's their company)
- Process: See demo → do PoC → decide. No committee. No procurement.
- Entry point: Often direct — LinkedIn, cold email, or word of mouth from another founder

---

## Attention Triggers (What Gets a Reply)

1. Another founder they respect used RWX: "ClickFunnels founder cut deploy cycles 14x — thought you'd appreciate the data."
2. Cost shock: "Most founders I talk to in [industry] are spending $X/month on CI/CD and not realizing it. Want to see where your money is going?"
3. Speed framing: "You're writing AI-assisted code now — your CI/CD is probably the bottleneck. Teams like yours are shipping 4–6x/day after switching."
4. Founder-to-founder credibility: "Dan (our CEO, ex-CTO of Root Insurance) built this to solve his own problem. Happy to intro you."

## What to Avoid

- Don't make it complex. One clear outcome per email.
- Don't ask for a long call — offer "15 minutes" or "free PoC, no commitment"
- Don't lead with features; lead with outcomes: cost, speed, engineer time saved
- Don't send feature sheets or decks first — they don't read them; they want to see it work
- Do name-drop Dan Manges (Root Insurance, Braintree) — founder-to-founder credibility matters
