# Persona: VP of Engineering

---

## Who They Are

**Titles:** VP of Engineering, VP of Platform Engineering, SVP Engineering
**Company size:** Typically 50–500 employees; 15–150 engineers reporting to them
**Background:** Former engineering manager or director; often technical but now primarily organizational
**Tenure at role:** 0–36 months (new VPs are the highest-signal buyers; they audit tools in first 90 days)

**ICP examples from our accounts:**
- VP of Platform Engineering at ClickFunnels (469 employees)
- Director of Engineering at PayNearMe (250 employees, 58 engineers)
- Director of Engineering at Verkada (2,000 employees, 539 engineers)

---

## What They Care About

### Primary Goals
1. **Shipping velocity:** Teams that ship more frequently and reliably are teams they can point to as wins
2. **Developer retention:** Engineers who are frustrated with tooling leave; happy engineers stay
3. **Infrastructure cost control:** CI/CD spend is visible on the P&L; reduces friction with CFO
4. **Reliability:** Flaky pipelines that block deploys make them look bad

### How They Measure Success
- Time-to-merge (from PR open to merge, ideally <2 hours)
- Deploy frequency (daily or better)
- Pipeline reliability (% of runs that complete without flake)
- CI/CD infrastructure cost per engineer per month

### Decision Criteria
- **ROI must be clear:** They need to justify the switch internally. Give them the numbers.
- **Risk must be managed:** Migration cannot break production deploys. Offer parallel running.
- **Team buy-in:** If engineers hate it, they'll hear about it. Developer experience matters.
- **Vendor stability:** They don't want to be calling dan@rwx.com at 2am. Mention SLA / support.

---

## How They Buy

- Initiates evaluation after: new role, headcount growth hitting CI/CD ceiling, cost audit
- Typical process: internal champion (Staff Eng or Platform team) does technical validation → VP signs off on budget
- Timeline: 30–90 days from first contact to close
- Budget authority: Usually $10K–$100K/year in their discretionary budget
- Often moves fast if there's a PoC with clear numbers

---

## Attention Triggers (What Gets a Reply)

1. A peer at a similar company with a quantified result: "Tropic's VP Eng cut CI/CD costs 67% — wanted to share the data."
2. A cost audit framing: "Most teams your size are spending $8–15K/month on CI/CD compute. I can show you where the waste is."
3. A productivity/morale framing: "The #1 engineer complaint at [comp size] companies is slow CI/CD. We fix that."
4. A specific signal: Mentioning they're on CircleCI or just raised a round.

## Outreach Hooks by Signal

| Signal | Opening Hook |
|---|---|
| CircleCI customer | "Most VPs I talk to on CircleCI are spending $10–15K/month and waiting 30+ min for builds. We cut both in half. Worth a look?" |
| New to role | "Congrats on the [VP Eng] role at [Company] — tooling audits in the first 60 days are table stakes. Happy to share how similar teams have cut CI/CD costs significantly." |
| Funding event | "Congrats on the [round] — infrastructure efficiency tends to come into focus right after a raise. We're helping [similar company] run CI/CD at [X]% lower cost." |
| Headcount growth | "Saw [Company]'s engineering team has grown quickly — CI/CD pain tends to compound at that pace. Teams your size typically save $3–6K/month with RWX." |

---

## What to Avoid

- Don't lead with technical features (DAG, content-based caching) — lead with business outcomes
- Don't ask for a long meeting; offer a 15-minute demo or free PoC first
- Don't make them do the work; offer to run the PoC for them
- Don't use phrases like "cutting-edge" or "enterprise-grade" — give them numbers
